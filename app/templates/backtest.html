What this does

Replays whichever parameters you pick below - bar by bar over real historical price data from your Kite connection. An entry fires the moment at least your chosen "Required" count of them agree on the same direction at once. Deliberately does not factor in Open Interest or VWAP - this tests your own combination of indicator and volume conditions on its own, separate from the broader "High-Conviction" filter on the dashboard. Results are broken out for Bullish and Bearish setups separately, since a strategy's edge often differs by direction.

Read before trusting the numbers: picking fewer required out of more selected parameters fires often and can produce hundreds of trades even over a short window - a high trade count makes the win rate more statistically meaningful, but still isn't a guarantee of future performance. Requiring most or all of your selected parameters fires far less often, so don't be surprised by a small trade count there. This is not investment advice - use it to sanity-check the rule, not as a promise of returns.

1 · Which gates earn their place?

Runs a baseline with every gate off, then one pass per gate with only that gate on, and reports what each did to your win rate — net of costs, on your own watchlist. Start here: it's the only tool that measures the filters you're actually running.

Measured in isolation, so it can't see two gates that only help together. Fewer trades isn't automatically worse, which is why trade count sits beside every win rate — a big delta on a handful of trades is noise. Gates that cannot apply to your scope are skipped and listed rather than reported as "no change", since never-tested and tested-and-useless are different findings.

2 · Does the BTST/STBT premise hold?

The overnight panel picks stocks that closed at the extreme of their range on a day that went their way, and holds them overnight. Nothing in this app could measure that trade until now — every other tool here holds for 5 to 20 bars (a fortnight on daily) and enters at the next bar's open, by which point the overnight move you were trying to catch has already happened.

This enters at the signal bar's own close and exits at the next open and the next close — the trade as you would actually take it. It applies only the two hard gates, not the soft checks, because the question is whether the core premise has an edge before any refinement. Refining a setup whose sign is wrong just produces a smaller loss more confidently.

Worth knowing what you might find: this setup buys the most extended point of the session, and short-horizon reversal is a well-documented effect — momentum tends to work over months, while over one to five days strong recent returns often give back. If the win rate lands meaningfully below 50%, the premise is inverted rather than merely weak, and no amount of tightening the other checks fixes it. A result near 50% after costs means there is no edge here either way.
