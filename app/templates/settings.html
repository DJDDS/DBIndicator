The screen

The five dials that actually change which stocks appear. Everything else on this page is either a filter you switch on and off, or an internal you can leave alone.

Out of RSI, MACD, Chaikin Money Flow and Relative Volume. Loosen this first if the screen is too empty.
Multiple of the 20-bar average volume needed to count as real participation.
Below this a stock is too quiet to be worth the risk. Watch the ATR column for a few days and set this near the bottom quartile of what you actually see - the default is a guess, not a measurement.
Past this you'd be chasing a move that already happened.
How near the day's high/low a close must be to count as decisive. Also the hard gate for the BTST/STBT panel.
The main dial for how short the shortlist is. A name must score at least this much on the evidence that was actually measurable for it. Raise it for fewer, higher-conviction names; lower it to see more. Scores are out of what could be measured, not out of a wished-for 100 — a row with no OI baseline never reaches this list at all.
A ceiling, not a target. Most days should come in well under it, and an empty shortlist is a valid answer — a screen that always returns the same number of names is sorting, not selecting.
How many of the overnight checks must actually hold. Every check listed can genuinely fail — the old panel counted several that were forced true by the same gates that admitted the row, so its scores had a floor of 6 and meant almost nothing.
Sends the day's overnight candidates once, at this time. 15:15 is the sweet spot — the daily bar's close position has essentially settled, and you still have fifteen minutes to place an order before the 15:30 bell. Earlier and you're reading an unfinished bar; later and you can't act.
On by default, unlike every other gate here — this is what links the OI read to the technical read, and it is the main reason the shortlist is short. It only ever rejects on ACTIVE disagreement: an absent OI baseline is unknown, not contrary, so it costs a row its place by leaving evidence unmeasured rather than by counting against it.

Risk & position sizing — does not affect which stocks appear

This app never touches your funds, positions or orders. Account capital is a number you tell it, used to turn the ATR stop into a suggested quantity. The daily limits feed an informational banner computed from trades you logged in the Signal Journal - nothing here blocks you from logging another trade past a limit.

1-2% is the typical F&O starting point.
Default 1.5 / 3.0 = a 1:2 risk-reward.
